# Understand the connections. Put the methods to work.

**Learning depth：** Quick overview

Explore three complementary methods, see why they connect, and use them to frame your own problem.

**Central thesis：** A problem map sets boundaries, an evidence chain tests claims, and an action loop brings new information.

## Core frameworks

### Problem map

Make boundaries, stakeholders, constraints and success criteria explicit.

Applies when: Entering an unfamiliar or poorly defined problem
Source: methods-demo.txt · Lines 3-4

### Evidence chain

Connect conclusions to observations and competing explanations.

Applies when: Judging whether a claim is well supported
Source: methods-demo.txt · Lines 6-7

### Action loop

Test an assumption, compare prediction and outcome, then revise.

Applies when: Turning understanding into a small informative action

A map helps identify which information is relevant.

- Record what belongs inside the problem.
- Choose an observable success criterion.

### Unit 2 Test the claim

Observations and explanations are not the same thing.

Key frameworks: Evidence chain

- Trace conclusions to observations.
- Seek competing explanations and counterevidence.
Source: methods-demo.txt · Lines 6-7

#### Evidence is not automatic proof

One observation may allow more than one explanation.

- Separate what was observed from what it might mean.
- Seek evidence that could challenge the current claim.
Source: methods-demo.txt · Lines 6-7

### Unit 3 Learn through action

A small test should produce information that can change the next decision.

Key frameworks: Action loop

- Test an important assumption at low cost.
- Compare results with predictions before scaling.
Source: methods-demo.txt · Lines 9-12

#### Feed results back into thinking

Results may update the evidence chain or the problem boundary.

- Revise assumptions when results differ.
- Return to the problem map when constraints change.
- These methods are not a guarantee of success.

# Frame the problem, test a claim, revise from results

A synthetic teaching example. The main path is one analytical planning order, not a mandatory sequence stated by the source. Conditions and feedback may change the appropriate next tool.

Goal: A scoped problem, a checked claim and an informative small test.

## Frame the problem

Evidence: Material evidence

Input: Problem description and current constraints.

Action / meaning: Record the boundary, stakeholders, constraints and an observable success criterion.

Output: An explicit scope and criterion.

Check: Check that the criterion is observable and the boundary is explicit.

methods-demo.txt · Lines 3-4

## Check the claim

Evidence: Material evidence

Input: A claim and its supporting observations.

Action / meaning: Separate observations from explanations and seek competing explanations and counterevidence.

Output: A traceable claim with its remaining uncertainty.

Check: Do not treat one observation as proof.

methods-demo.txt · Lines 6-7

## Run a small informative test

Evidence: Material evidence

Input: An important assumption and a prediction.

Action / meaning: Run a low-cost test, compare its result with the prediction and revise the assumption before scaling.

Output: New information for the next decision.

Check: Compare result and prediction before scaling.
